01 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

THE BRIEF / MY DESIGN IDENTITY

I am interested in graphic design, digital collage, photography and retouching, and character styling. I wanted a portfolio where these disciplines could be understood together, rather than presenting me as a specialist in just one area.

My goal was to combine a clear navigation system, expressive typography, selected projects, personal experience and a downloadable CV into one coherent professional identity. This website was developed over three weeks, with about 24 hours of practical work.

02 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

RESEARCHING STRUCTURE, NOT STYLE

During the first week I explored designers’ websites, paying particular attention to information architecture: how visitors find projects, background information and professional credentials. Two priorities emerged—clear navigation and a credible professional introduction.

These observations informed the relationship between Work, About, Experience and CV on my own site. I do not have a verified list of the websites originally visited in Week 1, so I have not attributed specific early decisions to named external examples.

LATER COMPARATIVE RESEARCH

W01

W02
VISUAL DESIGN & BUILD

Selected projects and portraits, organised the CV and biography, chose the typography, colours and hierarchy, then developed an initial version with ChatGPT-assisted coding.

Adjusted motion and interaction, fixed overlapping text and responsive issues, refined the biography and CV, collected peer feedback, and published the website using GitHub Pages.

04 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

MAKING THE IDENTITY LEGIBLE

I treated the complete site—not a single project—as the main evidence of who I am. The Work area shows visual outputs, About explains why I create, and Experience and CV provide a clearer professional context. Bold type and contrasting imagery help communicate personality, while navigation gives visitors a predictable way through the content.

05 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

FROM BREAKING LAYOUTS TO BETTER BRIEFS

The most visible technical problem was overlapping homepage text. The label and supporting text collided in the hero area, weakening readability and visual hierarchy. Other rounds of development involved Chinese typography, responsive layout, content updates and GitHub Pages deployment.

06 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

WHAT CHANGED IN MY PRACTICE

The clearest improvement was in how I communicate design requirements to AI. At first I was less specific about the results I wanted. As problems appeared, I learned to name the exact element, explain what was wrong and describe what should change. This made the cycle of feedback and testing more focused.

This is not evidence that I mastered front-end coding. Instead, the project strengthened my creative direction and problem-solving within an AI-assisted workflow. A limitation is that I did not systematically track every revision or benchmark accessibility, performance and user comprehension.

07 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

PEER FEEDBACK, WITH LIMITATIONS

A peer reviewed my website and gave a broadly positive response, without requesting particular changes. However, they did not describe the design identity or specific skills they perceived. I therefore cannot claim that this feedback conclusively validated my intended multidisciplinary positioning.

For a stronger evaluation next time, I would ask viewers what type of designer they think I am, which three skills they remember, and how easily they can find my projects and CV. This would turn general praise into more useful qualitative evidence.

09 / RESEARCH & REFLECTION

A PORTFOLIO THAT KEEPS GROWING

I plan to keep this website active by adding new work, refining interaction and responsive behaviour, and exploring web design and AI-assisted development more deeply. I also want the portfolio to become a practical resource for internship applications, professional opportunities and collaborations.

My next measurement approach will be simple: document each new release, record the problem and decision behind important changes, and ask visitors specific identity-and-navigation questions. Sustainable growth needs repeatable reflection—not only a polished final screen.
